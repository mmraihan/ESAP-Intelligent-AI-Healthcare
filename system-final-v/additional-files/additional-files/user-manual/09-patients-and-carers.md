# 9. Patients and carers

[← Manual index](README.md)

This chapter is written for patients and the people who help them. It uses plain language on
purpose.

**Your home page:** My health
**Menu:** My health · Tell us how you feel · Ready to share · My results · My records ·
*Access & people:* Share & consent · Care team · Family access · Settings

---

## What this service is, and is not

- It shows you **what your care team has shared with you**, in their own words.
- **You will never see a raw score from a computer.** Everything under *My results* was read
  by a member of your team, who then wrote a summary for you.
- It is **not** for emergencies. If something is happening right now, tell the nurse on the
  ward or call for help. Do not wait for a form.
- You decide who else can see your record, and you can take that access away at any time.

---

## Getting started

- **Your hospital set you up:** sign in with the details they gave you.
- **You are using ESAP on your own**, not linked to a hospital: create an account from the
  sign-in page (choose *patient*). Your home page then says you are using ESAP on your own,
  and you can check how you are feeling and prepare a summary for an appointment.

The first time, a **welcome** page asks two questions: **what language** you would like
(English or العربية) and **how much detail** you want (*Plain and simple*, *Standard* or
*Detailed*). You can change both later in **Settings**.

To change the language at any time, use the language switch in the top bar. In Arabic the
whole portal reads right to left, and the words you type to confirm an action are in Arabic
too: إيقاف instead of `STOP`, إزالة instead of `REMOVE`, إغلاق instead of `CLOSE`.

**Prefer to talk it through?** The chat button in the bottom-right corner opens an assistant
that takes down your details in your own words (*"Tell me what's going on…"*). A clinician
reviews everything, and it never shows you a score.

**Portal switched off?** If your home page says *"The portal is turned off for this hospital
right now"*, your hospital has paused the portal. Your record is safe; ask the ward or the
hospital for information in the meantime.

## Things you can do

### Tell your team how you feel

**Tell us how you feel** is a short set of questions, about five minutes:

1. **What is bothering you most?** Tap anything that fits, and describe it in your own words,
   the way you would tell a friend.
2. **When did it start?**, and whether it has been getting worse.
3. **Anything else you have noticed?** Things that seem unrelated are often the useful ones.
4. **What are you taking?** Including things bought without a prescription. If you do not know
   a name, describe it ("small white one for my heart" is genuinely useful). You can say if
   you have missed doses; nobody is checking up on you.
5. **What do you want to ask?** Your questions go on your summary so you do not forget them.

Use **Next** and **Back** to move through it and **Finish** at the end. You can stop and come
back; nothing is lost. Your answers go to your care team in your own words. They are never
turned into a score, and the form does not tell you what is wrong.

### Prepare for an appointment

**Ready to share** turns your answers into a one-page summary: what has changed, what you are
taking, and your questions.

- Switch off any section you would rather not share.
- **Download** it, or **Send to my team**. You can also just show your screen.
- **Change my answers** takes you back to the questions.

**What happens after you send it.** A member of your care team reads it on your hospital
record. The page then shows **What happens to what you send**: the date you sent it, and
either **Not seen yet** or **Seen by** *name* **on** *date*. Sending it does **not** run any
test or estimate and does not change any result. If something you wrote matters, your doctor
talks to you about it and updates your record themselves.

### Read your results

**My results** shows only what a member of your team has read and written for you. Each one
says who wrote it and when.

- **Ask a question about it** sends your question to your care team.
- **This does not describe me**: use it if a result seems to be about someone else. Your team
  treats this as urgent, because it may be someone else's record.
- If a result has been waiting a long time and nobody has spoken to you, **Ask my nurse about
  this**. You are entitled to chase it.

### See and download your records

**My records** lists your visit summaries, test results, scans, medicines and going-home
letters, including ones sent from another hospital.

- Filter: all, from this hospital, or from elsewhere.
- **Download** one, or download all of it. It is your record; you do not have to ask anyone
  or say why. There are two ways to download all of it:
  - **Download everything (PDF)** saves a printable copy you can read or take to another
    doctor: your hospital stays, the results your care team explained to you, your care team,
    the documents on your record, and who you have shared it with. It is always in English.
  - **Structured file** saves the same record as a data file (`my-health-record.json`) that
    another hospital's system can read.

  A single record downloaded on its own is saved as a data file too.
- **Request a record from another hospital**: say which hospital and roughly when.
- **Ask for a correction**: say what is wrong and what it should say.

### Decide who can see your record

**Share & consent** shows who has access now, and who actually looked.

- **Give someone access**: choose who (a family member or carer, a doctor or nurse elsewhere,
  another hospital or clinic, or a health app), how much they can see (visit summaries only,
  summaries and medicines, or everything), and until when (no end date, 30 days, 90 days or a
  year) → **Give access**.
- **Stop sharing**: type `STOP` to confirm. It takes effect immediately. Anything already sent
  to them stays with them; ask them directly if you want it deleted.
- **See the full history**: everyone who has looked at your record.

### Let a family member or carer help

**Family access** gives someone **their own login**, so you never share your password.

1. **Add someone** → their name, their email, how they are related to you, and what they can
   see: **Can see summaries**, **Can see everything** or **Can act for you**.
2. Leave **Tell me when they look at my record** on if you want a notification each time.
3. **Send invitation**. A box appears: *"Send (name) this link to set up their access."*
   There is no email service yet, so click **Copy link** and send it to them yourself.

**What the carer does:** they open the link, see **What you will be able to do**, enter their
name and **Choose a password** (at least 8 characters, typed twice), then click **Set up my
access**. They sign in to the portal with their own email and password, and a **People you
care for** card on their home page shows what you shared, labelled with how they are related
to you (for example *as Son or daughter*). They see only summaries your team released, never
a score. A link that has already been used or has been withdrawn shows *"This invitation
cannot be used."*

To end it: **Remove**, then type `REMOVE`. It happens immediately: the carer's **People you
care for** card no longer shows you.

### Contact your care team

**Care team** lists the people looking after you, taken from your hospital record, so it
always matches who is actually caring for you:

- the **attending physician** and **bedside nurse** named on each current stay. The one
  marked **leading your care** is the attending on your most recent admission;
- the **unit team** for each stay (for example *CCU team*): the doctors, nurses, pharmacist and
  case manager on that ward. They are the people who can read what you send;
- anyone outside the hospital who is part of your care, such as your family doctor.

When you move ward or are discharged, the list changes with your record. Nobody updates it by
hand.

**Send a message** to one of them. For anything urgent, tell the nurse on the ward instead. If
you are not happy with something, **Ask the patient liaison service to contact me**.

**Where your message goes.** Your doctors see your messages and questions about results on
your hospital record, alongside your **Ready to share** summaries and symptom reports. The
hospital's office also receives a copy and marks it handled.

**Things you've sent** (on the Care team page) lists every message, question and request you
have raised, with its status:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Received** | It has arrived and is waiting. |
| **Being looked at** | Someone is working on it. |
| **Handled** | It has been dealt with. If you still need an answer, ask your nurse. |

### Not linked to a hospital yet?

**Care team** has a **Connect with a hospital** card, mainly for people who created their own
account: *"Ask
a hospital to take you on. A clinician there accepts, and then you can share information both
ways."* Choose the **Hospital**, enter **Your age** and sex, and optionally **What brings you
in?**, then **Request to connect**. Your request shows as pending until a clinician there
accepts it. After that, what you send reaches that team and what they release appears under
**My results**.

## Settings

- **Language**, **Text size** (Normal, Large, Extra large), **Colour theme** (Light, Dark,
  System), **how much detail** we write in, **Reduce movement**, **Read pages aloud**.
- **Notifications**: when your team shares something new, when someone looks at your record,
  appointment reminders, and (off by default) occasional feature emails. Nothing about your
  health is ever in a marketing email.
- **Your account**: your name and email (ask your care team to change them, because they must
  match your hospital record) and **Change my password**. Changing it signs you out everywhere
  else.
- **Your data**: **Download my whole record** (takes you to **My records**, where you choose
  the PDF or the structured file) and **Download who has looked at it** (a CSV file). Both are
  free.
- **Sign out**, and **Close my account** (type `CLOSE`). Closing your account does **not**
  delete your hospital medical record, which hospitals must keep by law. Anyone you gave access
  to loses it immediately.

## Individual / Guest accounts

The permission rules also define an **Individual / Guest** role: a single self-serve seat that
can record and assess its own information. The sign-up page currently creates **Patient**
accounts, so in this build you will not meet this role.
