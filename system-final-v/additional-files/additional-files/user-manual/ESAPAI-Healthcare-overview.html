Product overview

A second opinion at the moment of admission.

ESAP is clinical decision support for hospitals. When a patient is admitted, it uses only what is known at that moment and gives the care team a ranked list of likely conditions plus four risk estimates, each explained and each checked for accuracy. A clinician reviews every estimate and makes the decision. People who are not in hospital can use it too: they describe their health problem and take a clear summary to a doctor.

Why this product

The first hours of an admission shape the whole stay

Within minutes of a patient arriving, the team has to decide what to look for, who needs closer watching, whether an ICU bed may be needed and when discharge planning should start. These decisions are made under time pressure, with incomplete information, for many patients at once.

The gap

Early risk is hard to see

Patients who will later need the ICU, stay long or come back within a month often look ordinary at the door. Their warning signs are spread across old records, home medications and a first set of labs that nobody has time to piece together.

The trust problem

Most clinical AI is hard to trust

Many tools give a score without a reason, are tested with information from later in the stay (so they look better than they are) and are not checked for fairness. Clinicians learn to ignore them.

The practical problem

Hospitals do not want another system

Hospitals are not replacing their EHR. They want focused tools that sit on top of what they already run, are quick to set up and do not add clicks or alarms.

ESAP's answer: one assessment at admission that gives five useful estimates, each with its reasoning shown, honestly tested, checked for fairness and reviewed by a clinician, running alongside the hospital's existing EHR.

What it does

Five questions answered at intake

The admitting team gets all five answers in seconds, from the information already collected at the door: demographics, how the patient arrived, vital signs, early labs, past diagnoses, home medications and the admission note.

M-01
What might the patient already have?

A ranked differential across ~500 standard condition categories (AHRQ CCSR).

M-02
In-hospital mortality

How often similar admissions did not survive to discharge.

M-03
ICU admission

How often similar admissions needed intensive care during the stay.

M-04
30-day readmission

How often similar patients came back within 30 days. Useful for early discharge planning.

M-05
Length of stay

A range in days (for example 4–8 days), not a single guess.

How to read a number. "Mortality 31%" means that of past admissions that looked like this one at admission, about 31 in 100 did not survive to discharge. It describes a group of similar patients, not a forecast for this one. ESAP says this on every results screen.

Not only for hospitals

Anyone can prepare for a doctor's consultation

You do not need to be admitted to a hospital to use ESAP. Anyone can create a free personal account, describe their health problem in their own words and take a clear summary to a doctor. Independent doctors, small clinics and telehealth doctors can use ESAP without a hospital too.

For the public

Describe your problem, then see a doctor well prepared

  1. Sign up with an email, in English or Arabic. No hospital link is needed.
  2. Tell us how you feel. Answer about five minutes of simple questions: what is bothering you, when it started, whether it is getting worse, other things you have noticed, the medicines you take and the questions you want to ask.
  3. Get a one-page summary of what has changed, what you take and your questions. You choose which parts to include.
  4. Share it with a doctor: download it, show it on your phone during the consultation, or send it to your care team once you are linked to a clinician or hospital.
  5. Keep control: decide who can see your information, and take access away at any time.
For independent doctors

The full clinical toolkit, without a hospital contract

  1. Self-serve sign-up for solo doctors, NPs, PAs, telehealth-only practices and small clinics. Ready the same day, with no IT project.
  2. A clinical attestation at sign-up confirms the user is trained to read risk estimates.
  3. The same five estimates and "Why?" explanations as the hospital version, for the doctor's own patients.
  4. Better consultations: the patient's own summary arrives before the visit, so time is spent on care, not on collecting history.
  5. A private account: each doctor's data is kept separate from everyone else's.

The admission-time cut

This is the rule that makes the numbers honest. A model that could see later test results or discharge notes would look far more accurate than it really is at the bedside. ESAP's data pipeline and every estimate enforce this rule.

Used by the modelsAge and sex, how the patient arrived, vital signs and labs taken at or before admission, past diagnoses, home medications, the admission note
Never usedLater labs, treatment response, later notes, discharge information. These are saved to the chart but never reach a model.

The models

The smallest model that does the job

Mainly well-tested gradient-boosted tree and linear models, chosen because their reasoning can be shown exactly. The length-of-stay model gives a range with 95% coverage. Larger AI models are added only when they measurably improve accuracy.

Explanations

Computed from the model, not written by AI

For the differential, each input's contribution is calculated exactly and adds up to the score. For the risk models, each input is removed in turn and the case scored again. A language model may put the result into one sentence, but it never invents the reasons.

Reliability

Refuses to serve wrong numbers

The system will not start if a model file is missing or the software versions differ from those used in training. Every result records exactly which model version produced it.

Built for clinical trust

Safety features that cannot be switched off

These features come with every plan and every deployment. A hospital cannot turn them off, and they are not sold as add-ons.

Explainable

A "Why?" panel on every estimate

It shows which inputs moved the number, in which direction and by how much. The explanation comes from the model itself, never from a text generator, so a clinician can check the reasoning.

Calibrated

Percentages that mean what they say

Of patients given 30%, close to 30% had the outcome. Each result carries a calibration badge. If key inputs had to be estimated, the badge is removed, not hidden.

Fair

No race, insurance or marital status in the models

These are recorded only so a fairness audit can check that the models perform evenly across patient groups. They never appear on a clinical screen.

Honest

Weaknesses are labelled

A model with a known limit carries a visible degraded label wherever it appears. Performance is reported separately for patients with and without prior history.

Human in charge

The clinician decides

Worklists stay in clinical order, not ranked by a model score. Acknowledging a result records that it was reviewed, not that anyone agreed with it.

Audited

Every access is logged

Every view, change, export and refused request goes to an append-only audit log that your privacy officer can search and report on.

Regulatory approach. ESAP is designed to qualify as non-device clinical decision support under the FDA's January 2026 CDS guidance: it supports a qualified clinician, shows the basis for every recommendation so it can be checked independently, and never shows patients a raw score.
Measured results

How accurate are the models?

Measured on patients the models never saw during training, from MIMIC-IV, a large de-identified hospital dataset (364,627 patients, 546,028 admissions). Every model is calibrated, reported separately for patients with and without prior history, and checked for data leakage.

ModelMain measureResultWhat it means
M-03 ICU admission AUROC 0.83
 Ranks patients who will need the ICU above those who will not in 83 of 100 random pairs. Meets its target.
M-02 Mortality AUROC 0.80
 Strong separation using admission data only. Work to reach the 0.87 target band continues.
M-04 30-day readmission AUROC 0.68
 In line with the published limit for predicting readmission at admission. Labelled degraded for first-time patients.
M-05 Length of stay Range accuracy 95%
 The predicted range contains the real stay for 95% of patients, as intended. Typical error is 2.9 days.
M-01 Differential Recall@20 Validation in progress Proof of concept complete. The full-scale run and leakage checks are finishing before we publish a headline number.

AUROC: 0.5 is a coin toss and 1.0 is perfect. Calibration error is 0.001–0.005 across M-02 to M-05, meaning the percentages closely match real outcomes. These are retrospective research results; performance at your hospital is measured during the pilot.

Why we publish our limits. Clinical AI tools are often shown with numbers that look better than they will perform at the bedside, because later information leaked into the test. ESAP has an automatic alarm for results that look too good and runs a control test before any such result is trusted. We would rather show you an honest 0.80 than a leaked 0.95.

Security & privacy

Privacy by design

How the product is built

  • Permissions are checked on the server for every request, with each role limited to its own patients or unit
  • Append-only audit log covering reads as well as changes
  • Each hospital's data is separate from every other hospital's
  • Records moving between hospitals require patient consent, and uncertain identity matches are never merged automatically
  • Patients control who sees their record and can withdraw access at any time
  • The research data used to train the models is kept completely apart from live patient data

Compliance programme

  • HIPAA / HITECH: ESAP acts as your Business Associate, with a BAA signed before any patient data is shared
  • A written FDA non-device CDS assessment for each model before it goes live
  • SOC 2 Type II, then HITRUST, on the path to general availability
  • GDPR requirements (legal basis, DPIA, transfer mechanism) resolved before any EU deployment

Certifications are in preparation. Their current status is shared openly during procurement.
